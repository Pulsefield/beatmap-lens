# Expert guide to mania pattern judgment

This guide records the reasoning supplied by the project's human expert on
2026-09-05 and the subsequent four-dimension Foundation approval. It is a working
local vocabulary. The reviewed distinctions are established guidance; the open
choices at the end are not approved definitions.
Source-specific decisions and original proposals remain in the local calibration
ledger, outside version control.

The consumer selects a small section vocabulary for an experiment independently
of its community map-tag targets. Use the distinctions in this guide to explain
arrangements even when only a few concepts are supervised.
Computed chord sizes, intervals, and occupancy are evidence, not extra labels for
experts to transcribe. A compositional description does not imply a hierarchical
model, automatic tag inheritance, or independently controllable latent factors.

## Learning-oriented campaigns

A section label supervises realized style, with evidence for its presence and
salience. A generation request asks for a style tendency; adherence strength and
gameplay-demand intensity are separate quantities. Do not interpret one of these
as a calibrated numerical value for another. Named coordinates can overlap and
correlate without forming independent controls.

Use chart actions, their causal history, and the declared canonical gameplay
profile. A chart-only review cannot establish audio-dependent mapping styles,
external presentation, provenance, or unobserved mapper intent. Defer concepts
outside the current experimental subset without deleting valid older observations.

Community votes are whole-difficulty observations. Declared compatibility may make
them weak aggregate evidence for a local concept, but a broad local predicate need
not recover a more specific community tag. For example, broad jack salience alone
cannot distinguish chordjack from longjack. Record the alignment direction, scope,
and pooling assumptions rather than inferring equivalence from shared names.

Exact chart facts and model-defined demand may contribute in parallel to style
recognition. These expert labels are not ground truth for numerical demand or the
moving gameplay frontier. They can help select informative histories and examples
for continuation experiments. Frontier claims require separately grounded responses
to controlled future actions; improved style classification alone is insufficient.

For evaluation, preserve unknowns, related sources, overlapping episodes, and
calibration exposure. Compare similar densities with different organizations and
the same organization at different rates. Successful style control requires a
recognizable semantic change in generated charts, beyond note count or difficulty.

## Read an arrangement at several levels

An attack event, a temporal relation, and a section characterization answer
different questions. Keep them distinguishable while composing a useful name.

| View | What to inspect | Useful descriptions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Events and occupancy | Which columns attack together, and which remain held? | Tap, short LN, sustained LN; jump = two simultaneous keys; hand = three |
| Temporal relation | What repeats, alternates, or moves across columns? | Same-column jack; alternating groups; ordered roll; release sequence |
| Extent and timing | How long does the organization persist, and at what intervals? | Two-attack minijack; long repeated run; fast or evenly spaced attacks |
| Local organization | How do those facts combine across the episode? | Jumpstream, jumptrill, long jump jack, chordjack, LN inverse |
| Expert characterization | What makes the arrangement tricky or coordination-focused? | Tech, LN coordination, LN release; independent salience |

These are analytical views, not approved exclusive ontology levels. Some concepts
span more than one view. LN inverse describes a hold/gap organization; Tech
evaluates a different property and can coexist with it.

Event descriptors explain the source. They do not require a separate annotation
or ground-truth label for every note or chord.

For example, **long jump jack** combines a two-key simultaneous attack group with
extended fast, evenly spaced repetition. Its chord size does not by itself make
the episode chordjack. A roll's speed does not make it a jack when successive
attacks move to different columns.

## Establish the episode before its label

Look at entry, continuation, and exit. Detect changes in attack-group membership,
alternation, roll direction, repetition length, rhythm, or occupancy. A rhythm
reset, isolated jump, or hand can separate two longer repeated passages. Preserve
that interruption instead of joining two runs through it.

The expert corrected an extended candidate into initial jumpstream, later
grouped alternation, and directional rolls with a reversal. The later Foundation
review explicitly included roll/burst in Stream organization and excluded the
grouped alternation, called jumpdrill in that review. Calling the entire crop
uniformly jumpstream or stream remains wrong because it crosses the excluded
episode. Different local witnesses somewhere inside a crop do not prove that
every label characterizes the whole crop.

A review window may show more than the claim. When an expert comments on context,
record the actual interval discussed rather than assuming they relabeled the
original scope. Keep approximate spoken cuts approximate; candidate source-row
boundaries are a separate proposed refinement. Do not invent numerical uncertainty
margins around an approximate time.

Local subpatterns can coexist: a roll ending in a hand can create a minijack in one
column. Record the localized relationship without calling the entire section
jack-focused. Keep related claims under the same source/episode grouping;
overlapping witnesses and alternative crops are not independent training samples.

## Tapping arrangements and jack distinctions

### Jumpstream

The reviewed positive is a stream-like succession of taps with recurring two-key
chords incorporated into the flow. Inspect the sparse single-note motion and the
jumps together. Neither a lone jump nor sustained alternation of two fixed
two-key groups establishes this organization.

Short LNs can enrich articulation without changing the basic tapping organization.
The expert described them as taps held a little longer. This interprets their role:
their source kind, duration, occupancy, and release remain LN facts. Do not erase
them, replace them with taps, or assume that LN presence requires an LN-focused label.

### Grouped jumpdrill (previously described as jumptrill)

In the reviewed 4K example, left two keys and right two keys repeatedly alternate
as groups. The expert called this jumpdrill in the final calibration and explicitly
judged Stream absent, using "salience 0". Record that as absence under the current
contract, not as a numerical salience extension. The sequence
`{1,2} → {3,4} → {1,2} → {3,4}` shows the distinction better than a note count.
This example establishes grouped alternation; do not infer that jumpdrill and
jumptrill are universal synonyms or invent every possible trill variant from it.
Keep the older wording in historical proposals and identify the revised local term.

### Roll and burst

A roll exposes ordered motion across columns. The approved local Stream
organization includes the reviewed roll/burst. The expert clarified that one group
contains exactly four notes moving in one direction across columns, with equal
spacing between adjacent notes and no
jump/chord relation formed between one burst and the next. Burst is the faster
roll description here; no numerical speed threshold was supplied. Keep the
direction and its alternation visible.

The clarification was: "一个roll/burst必须满足一组4个note, 两两等间距.
三组以上连续出现即显著." Thus three or more consecutive complete groups establish
prominent Stream organization in the local roll/burst episode. Count the complete
groups and inspect their joins; a direction change does not by itself supply
salience or erase separately established flow.

The reviewed source contains three complete descending groups and three complete
ascending groups, with two turning notes between these runs. Its 42/43 ms integer
gaps exactly match consecutive ticks of the source timing point's equally spaced
one-eighth-beat grid after flooring to source milliseconds. Keep that derivation
and the original timestamps separate; do not replace source times, invent a
general numerical tolerance, or describe unequal integer gaps as literally equal.
The source-backed group count and the expert's clarified rule resolve this
example to present/prominent, rather than supplying a default for every positive.

Fast ordered motion alone is not jack evidence. State inter-attack intervals and
column order; do not infer speedjack merely from density.

Check the join between directional groups: a reversal can repeat the last column
before moving away. A local minijack at that join can be real even when the main
organization is roll. Conversely, do not assume every reversal contains one.

### Minijack, longjack, and long jump jack

The expert used **minijack** for a short pair of consecutive attacks in one column.
It can occur alongside jumps or within a busier arrangement. A jump is two keys at
one time; a minijack repeats one key across two times. Do not confuse these counts.

For this local calibration, **longjack** means at least four fast, evenly spaced
repeated attacks. Count the run, show source intervals, and locate its reset.
The reviewed positive repeats a fixed two-key group: **long jump jack**. Two such
runs separated by a rhythm adjustment remain distinct runs. A two-attack minijack
does not become longjack because the crop contains many other notes.

The four-attack criterion is local expert guidance, not a universal community law.
No numerical threshold for "fast" or timing tolerance was supplied. Preserve
observed intervals and use calibrated comparisons; leave marginal cases unresolved.
Do not derive independent minijack samples from overlapping pairs inside one long
run. Describe a three-attack run factually without inventing an approved subtype.

### Chordjack

Do not use the shortcut "chords share columns across consecutive rows, therefore
chordjack." It mislabeled the reviewed fixed-group long jump jacks.

The expert's chordjack positive contains sustained three-key chord activity without
one column forming a longjack anchor. Compare the chord-group sequence and each
column's recurrence with the fixed repeated-group countercase. Alternating
left/right pairs are the reviewed jumpdrill and supply no consecutive same-column
jack relation. The later four-dimension review confirmed the changing-chord
positive as typical jack/chordjack, explicitly not longjack, while accepting the
fixed-group long jump jack as prominent broad Jack organization. The broad
positive does not erase this subtype distinction.

Whether absence of an anchor is a general necessary condition remains an explicit
question. Until settled, keep the reviewed positive/countercase and ask about
anchored borderline arrangements. Do not turn one example into a complete
definition, or assume that chordjack universally requires exactly three keys.

### Speedjack

The expert described speedjack through short inter-attack spacing requiring speed,
and called the reviewed minijack example non-typical. Brief run length alone is not
its definition. A long run is not excluded solely because it is long.

Its standalone target status is under reconsideration. Preserve established jack
structure, actual spacing, and expert typicality. Do not assert a speed threshold,
equate speedjack with minijack, or remove historical judgments while this is pending.

### Streams and limited evidence

The approved four-dimension Stream organization includes reviewed jumpstream and
roll/burst, and excludes the reviewed grouped jumpdrill. This supersedes treating
the earlier narrow streams critique as a blanket roll exclusion. Preserve all
local episode boundaries: a broader positive interpretation of rolls does not
make the intervening jumpdrill positive. Continuing activity and large note counts
remain insufficient.

A short example consisting mainly of jump jacks gave insufficient evidence for
the intended section assessment. That does not mean no pattern exists there, nor
justify converting insufficient evidence into an explicit absent label. Explain
what is visible and which broader judgment cannot be established.

## Long-note organization

### LN coordination

Inspect columns pressed and released at different times: for example, two occupied
columns while another LN starts, another column releases, or a tap occurs. The
expert's criterion concerns precise independent finger control across this
organization, not just many LN starts.

Use occupancy and event order to explain the interpretation without inventing a
measured player-demand curve. Preserve holds entering from before the claim;
they may explain why an event inside it changes the coordination.

### LN release

The discriminating feature is staggered release timing: different fingers release
at different moments. Compare columns releasing together and separately, including
other held notes at those moments.

The expert downgraded a proposed typical example because long LNs often ended
together with another LN. It can remain a weak positive: some staggered release
organization exists, but simultaneous endings reduce the independent-release
character. "Weak" is not uncertainty. It also does not automatically mean supporting
salience: ask whether the expert means example typicality, section prominence, or
both when that distinction affects the record.

An isolated pair starting and ending together does not establish LN release merely
because holds have ends. A cropped display edge is never a source release.

### LN inverse

The reviewed distinction is patterned gaps and renewals within a sustained hold
field. Inspect occupancy, release, gap, and renewed hold together. Successive
individual LNs handing off between columns, without that gap-based organization,
do not establish inverse. Keep complete starts of entering holds.

## Tech

The expert's local meaning is a tricky arrangement that disrupts a readily
understood regular pattern: attacks may be added or omitted relative to a locally
established expectation, rhythm may vary, or short LNs may alter articulation.
Explain the concrete sequence and comparison that create this character.

An "expected note" is an explanatory comparison with a regular motif, not an
assertion that the mapper omitted a required note. Do not fabricate an intended
original chart, errors, unseen musical events, or measured player difficulty.

Regular, easily controlled chordstream-like activity was explicitly judged not
Tech. Chords, note changes, LN presence, unfamiliarity, or failure to recognize
another pattern are insufficient. The corrected positive also had a narrower
onset: claim the interval where the tricky organization appears. A weak Tech
positive can coexist with an LN pattern.

This local concept is broader than a complex-snap-only catalogue description.
External identity, local definition, and alignment scope stay separate. The skill
does not authorize changing a task's frozen alignment metadata.

## Audit the inference, not just the reference

- Does scope stay within the organization described, or cross a reset, grouped
  alternation, roll, or other change requiring a narrower claim?
- Do witnesses show the full relationship, including intervening rows and entering
  holds, rather than just notes selected to fit the label?
- Are simultaneous chord arity, same-column repetition, group alternation, and
  ordered motion distinguished?
- Is the conclusion positive, an inspected negative, insufficient evidence, or an
  uninspected dimension? Are typicality and salience separate?
- Does the local pattern support the section characterization, or merely occur
  somewhere within a larger mixed crop?
- Does a reviewed countercase contradict the shortcut used to assign a broader tag?

Separate source/tool failures from semantic uncertainty. Ask about the specific
unresolved distinction rather than asking the expert to restate all pattern theory.
