# Reading perspectives

Use when the source arrangement remains unclear. The frozen Foundation owns
definitions; the harness supplies scoped human comparisons. This guide contains
no cached case verdicts.

## Follow relationships across scales

Inspect complete attack groups and their spacing together with holds and releases.
Ask what continues, what changes role, and where an episode enters or ends. Read
local motifs inside the surrounding phrase: constituent recognition alone does
not explain the whole, and successive episodes do not prove coexistence.

A selected core can leave other chord members in the background. Retain those
members and intervening rows when checking continuity or counterevidence. Preserve
the original scope; propose a boundary correction separately when it prevents a
sound judgment. Related or overlapping crops are not independent examples.

## Choose a perspective for the question

- **Repetition or exchange:** inspect adjacent attack groups, their exact column
  membership, interruption and resumption. A structural match locates an event;
  the episode and human comparisons determine its role and expression.
- **Flow or construction:** trace rhythm and articulation within cells and across
  joins. Both familiar motifs and changes can organize an episode. Compare the
  whole relationship with a human positive and counterexample when its meaning
  is unclear.
- **LN control:** inspect full hold durations, occupied columns and the order of
  tails and new attacks. Determine which holding/releasing roles persist and which
  tails follow through a brief attack. Use action/articulation views and human
  contrasts; raw overlap or a duration cutoff cannot supply the judgment.

Treat supplied statistics as source facts. Read a time-proportional image when
spatial arrangement helps; compressed row views do not preserve rhythm. Keep
source facts, human wording and your interpretation distinct when they disagree.
Chart-only evidence does not establish mapper intent or player-specific difficulty.
